關於現場、背景，還有那家小小咖啡廳

2025-11-25
關於現場、背景，還有那家小小咖啡廳 About the show, the setting, and that little caf? 這場 M2M 的演唱會，如果只看規模，其實比起之前去看的 Aimyon 算是迷你款。進場的隊伍就這樣乖乖繞一圈排隊。可是也因為如此，反而多了一種「真的在跟她們同一個空間裡呼吸」的溫度。 很幸運，手上的票券序號是 31，進場之後順利站到了舞台正前方的第一排。那個距離近到會看到她們互相對視的小表情。整隊入場、散場的流程其實算順，沒有太多混亂。唯一的小遺憾，是本來還以為會有周邊商品可以大買特買，結果現場只有媒體接待區，沒有任何一個讓人掏出錢包的攤位。只好把「買不到周邊」這件事，也當作是這場演出的限定記憶之一。 整個 band 的成員我幾乎都不認識，大概也叫不出名字。但從默契看得出來，他們應該是跟著兩位跑巡演的固定班底，在她們的國家或歐洲巡迴時，應該也是常常一起出現在舞台上的那種搭檔。每個人各司其職、沒有多餘的表演欲，卻精準地把所有該出現的聲音通通補上，讓這場「規模不大」的演出，聽起來一點都不廉價。 有趣的是，雖然早就開始照著歌單聽著歌，但這也導致演唱會進行到一半的時候，腦袋已經有點混亂——「這首到底剛剛唱過了，還是我只是自己在 Spotify 聽太多次？」網友問：「演唱會有唱〈Here I Am〉嗎？」卻無法很肯定地回答，因為大腦已經自動把現實演唱會和預習播放清單混剪成一支虛構 Live 版，只能說腦補功能有時候也太強大。 回到這次演唱會的初衷，這一切的當下，其實都與她們那個「戲劇性重逢」的小故事有關：兩位主角數十年沒有聯繫，有一天，剛成為人母的 Marion 因為車子爆胎，只好停留在一家咖啡廳等候處理；而後 Marit 走進了這家沒有其他客人的咖啡廳。那一瞬間就好像有一道從天而降的聚光燈，直直打在她們兩人身上。 於是有了這一趟巡迴，有了台北這一站，也有了我們這些早已從「美眉」升級成「媽咪」，或是升級成「上班族」的歌迷，再一次站在她們面前的機會。 也許，這次的巡演，在某種程度上，就是在替那段沒有正式道別的青春，補上一個新版結局。所謂 better endings，不一定是完美的大團圓，而是多年以後，大家都不再是當年的樣子，卻還願意再聚在一起，為彼此唱一次同一首歌。很幸運，我剛好坐在這個時空的觀眾席裡，一起見證。 About the show, the setting, and that little caf? If you only look at the scale, this M2M concert was definitely on the smaller side, especially compared with the Aimyon show I went to before. The queue simply circled calmly around the venue, everyone lining up in an orderly way. But maybe because of that, it felt even more like we were truly sharing the same air with them in the same room, rather than just watching something from a distance. I was quite lucky – my ticket number was 31, which meant that once we were let in, I managed to get a spot in the very first row, right in front of the stage. We were close enough to see their small, private exchanges of eye contact. The entry and exit were handled smoothly, with very little chaos. The only real disappointment was that I had expected a proper merchandise stand where I could happily spend too much money, but in the end there was only a media reception area and no place at all to buy anything. In the end, I decided to treat “going home without any merch” as a kind of limited-edition memory from this particular show. I hardly knew any of the band members by name, and I doubt I would recognise them on the street. But from the way they played together, it was clear they were the regular touring band – the kind of musicians who have probably shared many stages with Marit and Marion in their home country and across Europe. Everyone focused on their own role without trying to steal the spotlight, and yet they filled in every sound exactly where it was needed. Because of them, this “small-scale” concert never felt cheap or half-hearted at all. The funny part is that, although I had been listening through the setlist in advance for quite some time, that actually made things more confusing halfway through the concert. At some point my brain started asking: “Did they already play this one earlier, or have I just listened to it too many times on Spotify?” After the show, someone online asked, “Did they sing ‘Here I Am’?” and I honestly could not give a confident answer. My mind had already blended the real concert and my practice playlist into one imaginary live recording. It’s impressive how powerful our own mental “editing” can be. Coming back to the heart of this tour, all of these moments on stage are tied to their almost cinematic reunion story. After decades without being in contact, one day Marion – newly a mother – had a flat tyre and had to stop at a caf? to wait for help. That caf? happened to be completely empty, except for her. A little while later, Marit walked in through the door of the very same place. For a brief second, it must have felt as if a spotlight from above had suddenly switched on, shining directly on just the two of them. Because of that, we now have this tour, this stop in Taipei, and this chance for us – fans who have long since upgraded from “teenage girls” to mums, or to full-time working adults – to stand in front of them once again. Perhaps, in a way, this tour is about giving that part of our youth – the part that never got a proper goodbye – a new ending. Better endings do not always mean a perfect, dramatic happy-ever-after. Sometimes they simply mean that, many years later, none of us are who we used to be, but we still choose to gather again and sing the same songs for each other one more time. I feel very lucky to have been in the audience, in this particular moment in time, to witness it.
